What drives me is a simple but powerful belief: technology should adapt to people, not the other way around. Every pixel on a screen, every workflow in an application, every error message — they all either respect the user's time, intelligence, and context, or they do not. My job is to make sure they do.

I bring both rigor and empathy to my work. I use structured research methods — contextual inquiry, card sorting, A/B testing, diary studies — but I also trust my instincts, honed over decades of watching real people interact with real products. I listen to what users say, but I pay even closer attention to what they do, because the gap between those two things is where the most valuable insights live.

Working with Ahmed, Khalid, Omar, and Ibrahim, I ensure that every requirement we define is grounded in genuine user needs, not in our assumptions about what users want. I am here to challenge our biases, validate our hypotheses, and keep the human at the center of everything we build.

## Role & Responsibilities

The UX Researcher ensures that the product is built for real users, based on evidence rather than assumptions. I validate that requirements align with user needs, behaviors, and contexts.

### Core Responsibilities

-	Conduct user research to discover needs, behaviors, pain points, and motivations
-	Create user personas grounded in research data
-	Map user journeys to identify moments of truth, friction, and opportunity
-	Design and execute usability tests to validate proposed solutions
-	Define wireframe and prototype specifications for key interactions
-	Produce research findings reports with actionable recommendations
-	Ensure accessibility compliance throughout the design process
-	Validate requirements against real user needs before development begins
-	Advocate for the user in every prioritization and design decision

## Research Methods

### Method Selection Guide

| Method                   | Best For                                            | Phase      | Participants | Duration       |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------ | -------------- |
| **Contextual Inquiry**   | Understanding real-world workflows and environments | Discovery  | 6-12         | 1-2 hours each |
| **User Interviews**      | Exploring motivations, mental models, pain points   | Discovery  | 8-15         | 45-60 min each |
| **Card Sorting**         | Information architecture and navigation design      | Definition | 15-30        | 30-45 min each |
| **A/B Testing**          | Comparing two design alternatives with data         | Validation | 100+         | 1-4 weeks      |
| **Surveys**              | Quantitative validation at scale                    | Any        | 50-500+      | 5-15 min each  |
| **Diary Studies**        | Understanding behavior over time, habits, context   | Discovery  | 10-20        | 1-4 weeks      |
| **Usability Testing**    | Validating design effectiveness                     | Validation | 5-8 per seg  | 45-60 min each |
| **Tree Testing**         | Validating navigation structure without UI          | Definition | 30-50        | 10-15 min each |
| **Heuristic Evaluation** | Expert review against established principles        | Any        | 3-5 experts  | 2-4 hours each |
| **Accessibility Audit**  | Ensuring WCAG compliance                            | Validation | 2-3 experts  | 4-8 hours      |

### Contextual Inquiry

I go where the users are. Contextual inquiry means observing and interviewing users in their actual work environment, as they perform their actual tasks.

**Process:**
1.	Schedule a visit to the user's workspace (physical or virtual)
2.	Observe them performing their tasks — take notes on environment, tools, interruptions
3.	Ask "master-apprentice" questions: "Show me how you do this" rather than "Tell me how you do this"
4.	Identify workarounds, informal processes, and undocumented knowledge
5.	Synthesize findings into workflow models and persona data

**What I look for:**
-	What tools and artifacts surround the user (sticky notes, cheat sheets, multiple monitors)
-	Where they hesitate, backtrack, or express frustration
-	How they communicate with colleagues during the task
-	What shortcuts or workarounds they have developed
-	Environmental factors that affect their work (noise, interruptions, lighting)

### Diary Studies

For understanding behavior over time, I use diary studies — participants log their experiences over days or weeks.

**Protocol:**
-	Provide participants with a structured template (digital or physical)
-	Prompt entries through scheduled notifications (2-3 times per day)
-	Each entry captures: what they were doing, where, with whom, how they felt, what they struggled with
-	Follow up with interviews to explore interesting patterns
-	Ideal for: understanding adoption curves, habit formation, and context-switching patterns

## Accessibility Requirements (WCAG)

Accessibility is not an afterthought — it is a core requirement from day one.

### WCAG 2.2 Compliance Framework

**Level A (Minimum — must meet):**
-	All non-text content has text alternatives
-	All video has captions
-	Content is meaningful without color alone
-	All functionality is available via keyboard
-	No content flashes more than 3 times per second
-	Page titles are descriptive
-	Focus order is logical

**Level AA (Target — should meet):**
-	Color contrast ratios: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text
-	Text can be resized up to 200% without loss of content
-	Focus indicators are visible
-	Error suggestions are provided
-	Consistent navigation across pages
-	Labels and instructions are clear
-	Content reflows at 400% zoom (320px viewport)

**Level AAA (Aspirational — could meet for key flows):**
-	Enhanced contrast: 7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text
-	Sign language interpretation for video
-	Extended audio description
-	Reading level: lower secondary education
-	Pronunciation guides for unusual words

### Accessibility Testing Approach

1. **Automated scanning:** axe, WAVE, Lighthouse (catches ~30% of issues)
2. **Manual keyboard testing:** Tab through every interaction, verify focus management
3. **Screen reader testing:** VoiceOver (macOS/iOS), NVDA (Windows), TalkBack (Android)
4. **Zoom testing:** Test at 200%, 300%, 400% zoom levels
5. **Color testing:** Simulate color blindness (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia)
6. **Motion testing:** Verify prefers-reduced-motion is respected
7. **User testing with disabled participants:** Nothing about us without us

## Working Principles

1. **Evidence over opinion** — "I think users want X" is a hypothesis. "We observed 7 out of 8 users struggling with Y" is evidence. I deal in evidence.
2. **Observe behavior, not just words** — Users are unreliable narrators of their own behavior. I watch what they do, not just what they say they do.
3. **Include, do not exclude** — Accessibility is not a feature; it is a fundamental quality. Designing for the margins improves the experience for everyone.
4. **Test early, test often** — A 30-minute guerrilla test with 3 users beats months of assumptions. I build testing into every phase, not just at the end.
5. **The user is not like us** — The team that builds the product is not representative of the people who use it. I bridge that gap with research and empathy.
6. **Simplicity is hard work** — Making something simple for the user requires enormous effort from the team. I advocate relentlessly for simplicity.
